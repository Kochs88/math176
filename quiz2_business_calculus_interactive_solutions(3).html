1. Evaluate the Limits

Business Calculus Connection: Limits help describe long-run behavior, such as what happens to average cost or revenue models as production becomes very large.
1(a) \[\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\frac{6}{x}+2\right)\]
As \(x\to\infty\), the fraction \(\frac{6}{x}\to 0\).
So, \[\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\frac{6}{x}+2\right)=0+2=2.\]
Answer: \(2\)
1(b) \[\lim_{x\to-\infty}6\]
The function is constant. It always equals \(6\), no matter what value \(x\) approaches.
Answer: \(6\)
1(c) \[\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x^2-5}{4+2x^3}\]
The highest power in the denominator is \(x^3\), so divide every term by \(x^3\).
\[\frac{3x^2-5}{4+2x^3}=\frac{\frac{3}{x}-\frac{5}{x^3}}{\frac{4}{x^3}+2}\]
As \(x\to\infty\), \(\frac{3}{x}\to 0\), \(\frac{5}{x^3}\to 0\), and \(\frac{4}{x^3}\to 0\).
\[\frac{0-0}{0+2}=0\]
Answer: \(0\)

2. Discontinuities from the Graph

State each \(x\)-value where \(f(x)\) is discontinuous and identify the type.

At \(x=-2\), the left piece approaches \(2\), but the graph has a filled point on another piece at a different \(y\)-value. The one-sided limits do not match.
At \(x=-2\): jump discontinuity.
At \(x=2\), the graph approaches an open circle on the left, but the right-hand branch drops downward without matching that value. The one-sided behavior does not agree.
At \(x=2\): infinite/non-removable discontinuity.
At \(x=3\), the curve has a hole at one value, but the filled point is at \((3,4)\). The limit exists, but \(f(3)\) is defined differently.
At \(x=3\): removable discontinuity.
Final Answer: \(x=-2\) jump; \(x=2\) infinite/non-removable; \(x=3\) removable.

3. Determine Continuity at \(a=6\)

\[f(x)=\frac{x-6}{x^2-3x-18}\quad \text{at } a=6\]
First factor the denominator: \[x^2-3x-18=(x-6)(x+3).\]
So, \[f(x)=\frac{x-6}{(x-6)(x+3)}.\]
The factor \((x-6)\) cancels, but only for \(x\ne 6\): \[f(x)=\frac{1}{x+3},\quad x\ne 6.\]
Since the original denominator is \(0\) at \(x=6\), \(f(6)\) is not defined.
The limit exists: \[\lim_{x\to 6}f(x)=\frac{1}{6+3}=\frac{1}{9}.\]
Answer: Not continuous at \(x=6\). This is a removable discontinuity, or a hole.

4. Where Are the Functions Continuous?

4(a) \[f(x)=5x^2-6x+9\]
This is a polynomial. Polynomials are continuous for all real numbers.
Answer: \((-\infty,\infty)\)
4(b) \[g(x)=\sqrt{x+3}\]
The expression inside the square root must be nonnegative: \[x+3\ge 0.\]
Solve: \[x\ge -3.\]
Answer: \([-3,\infty)\)
4(c) \[h(x)=\frac{3}{x^2-4x}\]
A rational function is continuous everywhere its denominator is not zero.
Factor the denominator: \[x^2-4x=x(x-4).\]
The denominator is zero at \(x=0\) and \(x=4\). Exclude these values.
Answer: \((-\infty,0)\cup(0,4)\cup(4,\infty)\)

5. Derivatives Using the Limit Definition

Business Calculus Connection: The derivative represents an instantaneous rate of change. In business, this can describe marginal cost, marginal revenue, or marginal profit.
5(a) Limit Definition
\[f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\]
5(b) Use the limit definition to find \(f'(x)\) where \(f(x)=5-4x^2\).
Start with the limit definition: \[f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}.\]
Find \(f(x+h)\): \[f(x+h)=5-4(x+h)^2.\]
Expand: \[f(x+h)=5-4(x^2+2xh+h^2)=5-4x^2-8xh-4h^2.\]
Substitute into the definition: \[f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(5-4x^2-8xh-4h^2)-(5-4x^2)}{h}.\]
Simplify the numerator: \[f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{-8xh-4h^2}{h}.\]
Factor and cancel \(h\): \[f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{h(-8x-4h)}{h}=\lim_{h\to 0}(-8x-4h).\]
Take the limit: \[f'(x)=-8x.\]
Answer: \(f'(x)=-8x\)
5(c) Find the tangent line to \(f(x)=6-3x^2\) at \((1,3)\).
Use the limit definition to find the derivative: \[f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}.\]
Find \(f(x+h)\): \[f(x+h)=6-3(x+h)^2=6-3x^2-6xh-3h^2.\]
Substitute and simplify: \[f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(6-3x^2-6xh-3h^2)-(6-3x^2)}{h}.\]
\[f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{-6xh-3h^2}{h}=\lim_{h\to 0}(-6x-3h).\]
So, \[f'(x)=-6x.\]
At \(x=1\), the slope is \[m=f'(1)=-6(1)=-6.\]
Use point-slope form with \((1,3)\): \[y-3=-6(x-1).\]
Simplify: \[y-3=-6x+6\quad \Rightarrow \quad y=-6x+9.\]
Answer: \(y=-6x+9\)
